Our daily lives shape our health

Our health is shaped over time by many factors, including genetics, our daily habits, our experiences and relationships, the environments where we live, learn, work, and play, and our access to quality care.

No single factor explains chronic disease. But understanding the many influences on health can reveal opportunities for prevention throughout everyday life.

Prevention creates possibility

Chronic disease is complex. Genetics can play a role, but many other factors also contribute to health and disease over time. That creates meaningful opportunities for prevention throughout our lives.

Prevention matters at every stage of health, including after a diagnosis. It can mean reducing the risk of disease before it develops, identifying risks or disease earlier, delaying the onset or progression of disease, and reducing complications or improving health for people already living with chronic conditions. Prevention also extends beyond individual choices. The environments around us, the institutions we interact with, and our access to quality care can all help make healthier lives more possible.
